# Workflows

Step-by-step guides for common knitting tasks using pyKnit tools.

## Plan a sweater from scratch

```
1. Yarn Advisor          → Choose yarn weight and fiber
2. Needle Advisor        → Choose needles for your yarn
3. Knit gauge swatch     → Measure stitches and rows per inch
4. Raglan Sweater        → Enter gauge and measurements, get full plan
5. Follow instructions   → Cast on and knit!
```

### Measuring yourself

- **Neck circumference** -- Measure around your neck where the collar sits
- **Bust/chest** -- Measure at the fullest part
- **Upper arm** -- Measure around the fullest part of your upper arm
- **Wrist** -- Measure around your wrist
- **Body length** -- Measure from underarm to where you want the hem
- **Sleeve length** -- Measure from underarm to wrist

### Ease

- **Positive ease** (0 to +4 inches) -- Garment is larger than body measurement
- **Negative ease** (-2 to 0 inches) -- Garment is smaller, hugs the body
- **Typical ease for sweaters:** +2 to +4 inches for a relaxed fit

## Knit custom socks

```
1. Measure your foot       → Note foot circumference and length
2. Knit gauge swatch       → Measure stitches and rows per inch
3. Sock Calculator         → Enter measurements, get sock plan
4. Follow the plan         → Cast on and knit each section
```

### Foot measurements

- **Circumference at top** -- Measure around ankle above the instep
- **Circumference of ankle** -- Measure at the ankle bone
- **Length from top to heel** -- Ankle to bottom of heel
- **Length from heel to toe** -- Heel curve to tip of toe

### Negative ease

Socks should fit snugly. Use 10-20% negative ease for a comfortable fit.

## Resize a pattern to fit you

```
1. Knit gauge swatch              → Measure your gauge
2. Compare to pattern gauge       → Enter both gauges
3. Gauge Conversion               → Convert pattern measurements to your gauge
4. Even Shaping                   → Space any increases/decreases evenly
```

### Example

Pattern gauge: 27.5 sts / 10 cm (2.75 sts/in)
Your gauge: 23.5 sts / 10 cm (2.35 sts/in)
Pattern says: knit to 42 inches

Gauge Conversion tells you: knit to 49.4 inches instead.

## Estimate yarn for a project

```
1. Yarn & Time Estimator  → Select project type and dimensions
2. Enter your gauge       → Stitches and rows per inch
3. Read results           → Yards, grams, balls, and time estimate
```

### Tips

- Add 10-20% extra yarn for colorwork or complex patterns
- Buy an extra ball if you're close to the limit
- Estimate is based on average yarn usage per stitch

## Plan a pi shawl

```
1. Pi Shawl         → Enter radius and row gauge
2. Read increase rounds → Know exactly where to double stitches
3. Knit!            → Follow the increase schedule
```

### Pi shawl math

A pi shawl doubles stitches at geometric intervals:
- Row 1: Cast on
- Row 2: Double
- Row 4: Double
- Row 8: Double
- Row 16: Double
- (Continue doubling at 2^n)

Between doubles, you knit even (no increases).
